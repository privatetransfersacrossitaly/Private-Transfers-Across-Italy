# Milan cluster — research log

Every factual claim the Milan/Lombardy pages make, the source it came from,
the date it was checked, the pages that depend on it, and the caveat attached
to it.

**All dates checked: 2026-10-26** unless stated otherwise.

Same rule as Rome: if a claim is not in this table, it does not go on a page.
Where sources disagreed, the disagreement is recorded rather than resolved by
picking the convenient number.

> **Revised at the final review, 2026-10-26.** Four entries overstated where a
> claim was used — the pages were more conservative than this log said — and
> one claim that appeared in the project's own documentation was never in this
> log at all. Both kinds of defect are corrected below, and §8 records the
> second. A research log that misdescribes the pages is worse than none,
> because it is the thing an auditor trusts.

| # | Claim | Used on | Caveat |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | Milan has two distinct zones, not one: Area B, a low-emission zone covering most of the city, and Area C, a charged zone inside it covering the Cerchia dei Bastioni | milan, milan-stations, route pages | The nesting is the point — most explanations elsewhere conflate them |
| A2 | Area C operates all year, Monday to Friday, 07:30–19:30, public holidays excluded | milan | **Time-sensitive.** Dated in the visible copy |
| A3 | Area B operates Monday to Friday, 07:30–19:30, and is not active on Saturdays or public holidays | milan | **Time-sensitive.** Dated in the visible copy |
| A4 | Area B restricts the most polluting vehicles, goods vehicles over 12 m, vehicles carrying dangerous goods, and vehicles without blind-spot warning systems | milan | — |
| A5 | Area B is bounded by several hundred camera-controlled access gates | **number not used** | The published count has changed (186, previously 188). The page says "a few hundred" and gives no figure, which is the safer form |
| A6 | Taxis whose plates are registered for ZTLs and reserved lanes are exempt from the Area C charge | milan | From the city's own support centre |
| A7 | Private-hire cars (NCC) of up to nine seats, with plates registered for ZTLs and reserved lanes, are exempt from the Area C charge | milan | From the city's own support centre. **A statement about a vehicle category, not about any particular journey or operator** |
| A8 | Vehicles longer than 7.5 m need a specific derogation for the Cerchia dei Bastioni | **not used** | Verified, but irrelevant to the vehicle sizes these pages concern. Recorded only |

**Deliberately not quoted:** the Area C daily charge. It has changed (reported
as rising from €5 to €7.50) and a stale fee on a transfer page is worse than
no fee. The pages say a charge exists and link to the city.

**Re-verified at the final review, 2026-10-26.** Area C's hours were confirmed
directly against the Comune di Milano's own Area C page, which carries a June
2026 update and states Monday to Friday, 07:30–19:30, public holidays
excluded. **A2 is re-verified.**

**A3 (Area B) could not be re-fetched.** Both Comune support-centre URLs now
answer HTTP 403 to an automated request. The hours are unchanged from the
original check earlier the same day and are corroborated by ATM, which
publishes the same Monday-to-Friday 07:30–19:30 window — but ATM's page
carries a staleness indicator, so this is corroboration rather than
confirmation. The page's copy already dates the claim and links to the city,
which is the right treatment for a fact in this state. **Classification:
verified but time-sensitive; re-confirmation blocked.**

**Still the most volatile facts in this log**, as the ZTL hours were in the
Rome cluster. Both are set by city ordinance.

**The rule this section exists to enforce:** a road transfer is a road
transfer. No page implies that a boat, a ferry crossing or a lake excursion is
included in one. Where a journey would need a ferry, the page says so and
sends the reader to the operator.

## 7. What no source could establish

- Which Milan or Lombardy routes, vehicles and meeting arrangements the
  business can actually provide. No first-party operational record exists, so
  every page is framed as an enquiry subject to confirmation.
- Whether cross-border service to Switzerland can be offered at all.
- Any price, journey time or distance beyond the two the operators publish
  (Bergamo's ~5 km and ~50 km).
- Whether any venue, hotel, airline or ferry operator has any relationship
  with this business. None is implied anywhere.

## 8. A claim that was never in this log — and has been removed

Found at the final review, 2026-10-26.

| Claim | Where it appeared | Status | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| "**Half** its arrivals are going into Milan; a great many of the rest are going north to the lakes" | Header comment in `content/milan/malpensa.ts` | **Unsupported.** No source in this log, or found since, establishes how Malpensa's traffic divides by destination | Removed. No replacement statistic invented |
| "For **a great many** of the people who land here, it is not the beginning of a journey into Milan at all" | Malpensa hero lead, EN and IT (rendered copy) | Same claim, softer wording, same absence of evidence | Replaced with geography: Milan is south-east of the airport, the lakes are north of it, so for a lake-bound traveller the city is a detour |
| "For **many arrivals** this is the real destination" | Malpensa destination matrix, EN and IT | Unsupported proportion | Replaced with direction: "away from Milan, which lies south-east of the airport" |
| "It is among the **most common requests** from this airport" | Malpensa FAQ and the Malpensa→Lake Como route FAQ, EN and IT | Operationally unverified — a claim about this business's own enquiry mix, for which no first-party record exists | Replaced with the geographic reason the journey makes sense |
| "**A great many** people landing at Malpensa are not going to Milan at all" | Malpensa→Lake Como hero lead, EN and IT | Unsupported proportion | Replaced with "Not everyone who lands at Malpensa is going to Milan" — true, and claims no quantity |
| "The road up this shore is the one **most airport transfers** use" | Lake Como, Menaggio entry, EN and IT | Unsupported claim about transfer volumes | Replaced with geography: it is the shore nearer the airport |
| "**The most common way** people arrive" | Lake Como onward index, EN and IT | Unsupported | Replaced with a plain description of the journey |
| "The five **asked about most**" / "the journeys **people ask about most**" | Lake Como town index and the Milan hub's onward section, EN and IT | Operationally unverified | Replaced with "five of the best known" and "the journeys that have pages of their own" |

**The replacement principle.** Every substitution states geography or page
structure, both of which are verifiable, in place of traveller volumes, which
are not. The airport's position relative to Milan and the lakes does the
explanatory work the invented proportion was doing, and does it honestly.

**Not corrected, because it is sourced:** Linate's "the one most of its
passengers use repeatedly rather than once" paraphrases SEA's own description
of its customers as mostly frequent flyers (M7). It is the operator's claim
about its own traffic, attributed on the page to the operator.

**Flagged for the owner, out of scope here:** the Rome cluster uses the same
"journeys people ask about most" framing in two places. It is the same class
of unverified operational claim. Changing signed-off Rome copy was outside
this audit's scope, so it is recorded rather than altered.

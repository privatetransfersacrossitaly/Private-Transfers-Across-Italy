## 2. Publishable now

### Caserta — the strongest candidate

`reggiadicaserta.cultura.gov.it/en/how-to-get-here/` was read today and gives, verbatim:

- **"The Museum does not have its own public parking."**
- Main access **"from Piazza Carlo di Borbone"**; the Royal Park and English Garden also
  **"enter from Corso Pietro Giannone, 5."**
- **"Take the A1 motorway and exit at Caserta Nord. Proceed following the signs for
  Caserta."** and **"If you arrive via the A30 motorway, exit at Caserta Sud, then follow
  the signs for Caserta."**
- Footer address **"Piazza Carlo di Borbone 81100 Caserta CE"**.
- The station is across the square, reachable on foot.

**Not published, so not claimable:** any drop-off arrangement for cars, any named car
park (the only named parking option is a camper stop at Bosco di San Silvestro), and any
waiting place.

This is a complete, defensible page in the house style: a named publisher, a direct quote
that is useful precisely because it is negative, and a declared gap where the drop-off
would be.

**One conflict to settle first.** Pages on the same official site give the Giannone
entrance as number **5** and number **17**. The "how to get here" page says 5. Resolve it
before publishing, or publish the main entrance only — do not average them.

### Pompeii — publishable, one step behind Caserta

`pompeiisites.org/en/visiting-info/how-to-get-there/`, read today, gives the three
entrances **with their street addresses**, which Phase 1 could not confirm:

- **"Porta Marina (via Villa dei Misteri)"**
- **"Piazza Esedra (piazza Porta Marina Inferiore)"**
- **"Piazza Anfiteatro (piazza Immacolata)"**
- By car: **"Motorway A3 Napoli-Salerno (exit Pompeii Scavi)."** No further road
  directions are given.

Two things remain unstated and must stay unstated: **the page does not say whether any
entrance is reachable by vehicle**, and it gives no taxi arrangement, coach drop-off or
car park.

The park's FAQ answers parking directly — **"No. Outside the Archaeological Park there are
many municipal parking spaces available at a cost, delineated by blue lines. There are
also several private car parks."** — but that page carries a publication date of
**29 May 2018** and no last-updated field.

So Pompeii ships on the same terms Florence did: the fact is quoted **with its date
attached**, presented as what the park said in 2018 rather than as a current arrangement.
That is honest and still useful. What it cannot do is let a 2018 statement read as today's
parking situation.

**Positioning constraint, from our own homepage.** The site states twice that **"We drive.
We are not a tour operator, we do not sell tickets or admissions"**. Pompeii and Caserta
are therefore **transfer-endpoint pages** — where a car can go, and what the site does not
publish about it. No opening hours, no ticket prices, no visit advice, no itinerary.

## 3. The Amalfi Coast block, and the gate in front of it

### The gate: SS163 alternating plates

This is the single most important access fact for Sorrento, Positano, Amalfi, Ravello and
Salerno, because it governs whether a private vehicle may use the coast road at all on a
given day.

**It could not be verified today.** `stradeanas.it` **refused the connection twice**
(`ECONNREFUSED 185.121.36.88:443`) for both the SS163 limitations notice and the tourist-coach
notice. What secondary outlets report, none of it confirmed:

- Ordinances **337/2019 and 340/2019**, applying — unless revoked — **1 April to
  30 September each year**, covering the SS163 between **Vietri sul Mare and Positano**,
  **10:00 to 18:00**, with motorcycles excluded throughout.
- ANAS's stated purpose was to harmonise restrictions the coastal municipalities had each
  adopted; ANAS later **suspended** the scheme on certain stretches at councils' request.
- **2026 calendars conflict outright between outlets** — one gives weekends and holidays
  in June and July, daily in August and September; another says 1 June to 31 October;
  a third gives spring windows entirely.
- A **taxi and NCC exemption is widely reported and entirely unverified**, including what
  documentation a driver would have to produce at a check.
- The most recent ANAS material located anywhere is from **2023**.

**Do not publish any of it.** Not the ordinance numbers, not the hours, not the calendar,
and above all not the NCC exemption — an exemption we cannot evidence is exactly the claim
that puts a passenger in a stopped car.

Two honest ways forward: retry ANAS directly (it may simply have been unavailable today,
and the ordinance PDFs are said to be listed on its Campania SS163 page), or write each
coast page with the restriction named as **real but unquantified**, pointing the reader at
ANAS and the Comune. The second is publishable today; the first is better.

**Sorrento is not behind this gate.** The reported scheme runs Vietri sul Mare–Positano on
the SS163; Sorrento sits on the Sorrentine Peninsula and is reached via the **SS145
Sorrentina**, which ANAS treats under a separate limitations notice. Sorrento can therefore
proceed independently of the SS163 question — one reason it outranks the rest of the block.

### Sorrento — rank 3

`comune.sorrento.na.it` is reachable and carries a real, citable ZTL:

- Council regulation **n. 7 of 16 March 2023**, with a further regulation document
  (DCC 28-2023) republished **03/07/2025**.
- Permits issued under **Ordinanza n. 230 del 16/06/2023** "e successive modifiche",
  requestable through an online service.
- A seasonal period — **1 April to 25 October**, plus 7 December to 6 January limited to
  holidays and pre-holidays — with weekday hours 19:30–04:00 and an additional
  10:00–13:00 window on holidays. **Older notices give different windows**, so the current
  ordinance must be read rather than the news page.
- Annual permits expire 31 December; **temporary permits** for documented needs can be
  requested online or by calling a green number while passing through.

That last item is the commercially meaningful one for a transfer business, and it is the
kind of fact that justifies a page. It needs the ordinance text read before anything is
published, because the hours already disagree across the Comune's own pages.

### Positano — rank 4, highest stake

`ztlpositano.it` was confirmed today as **official** — run by the Comune di Positano
through its ZTL service and the Polizia Municipale (Via G. Marconi n. 111). The landing
page carries **no rules**, but links a dated **"GUIDA PORTALE ZTL — ZTL PORTAL GUIDE"
(20-05-2026)** in Italian and English, and gives a Polizia Municipale email.

Secondary sources describe a ZTL that is **active all day, every day** with no time bands,
covering Via Pasitea – Piazza dei Mulini – Via C. Colombo, and a **charge that applies to
vehicles with more than five seats** — €5,00 per transit, citing **D.G.C. n. 37/2024** —
paid online via PagoPA after registration validated by the Polizia Municipale.

**If that seat threshold is real, it is the most commercially relevant fact in the whole
cluster**: a minivan on a Positano job would be chargeable where a saloon would not. Which
is precisely why it cannot be published from a tourist website. Read the official guide,
then write the page.

### Amalfi — rank 5

`comune.amalfi.sa.it` is reachable and holds the primary material: a full
**Regolamento della viabilità nel centro storico** (PDF), a ZTL access-and-parking norms
document, and the ZTL authorisation request form.

- Verbatim from the regulation: **"E' fatto divieto di transito in Piazza Flavio Gioia e
  Lungomare dei Cavalieri dalle ore 10:00 alle ore 04:00 del giorno seguente per tutti gli
  autocarri."** That is a goods-vehicle ban, not a car ban — useful context, not our rule.
- Loading and unloading inside the ZTL requires prior authorisation from **Amalfi
  Mobilità**.
- **The permit regime changed on 1 January 2026**: all existing green, red and white
  permits were to be revoked at the end of the previous year, with applications made
  online through **Sistema Amalfi** from 17 November 2025.

That last point makes Amalfi time-sensitive in a good way — the current regime is new and
documented. **A 2011 notice giving Flavio Gioia car-park tariffs (€5,00/hour) exists and
must not be used**; it is fifteen years old.

### Ravello — rank 6

`comune.ravello.sa.it` is reachable. The Comune lists its ZTL zones, including
**"ZTL Via Giovanni Boccaccio (con inizio dal tratto sottostante il tunnel che conduce a
Piazza Duomo) Piazza Duomo"**, requires authorisation displayed under art. 124 of the
Codice della Strada, and runs permits through **ravello.webpass.it** and a service branded
**ZetaPass**. The most recent ordinance number located is **n. 7 del 14/03/2025**, cited on
the electric-cart page; the PDF was not opened.

Access at the Piazza Duomo car park is by **automatic plate recognition at a barrier**,
and a renewal deadline of **31 January 2026** applied to ZTL transit and parking permits —
explicitly including hotels and owners of parking areas inside the ZTL. Useful, and all of
it needs the ordinance read first. Material from 2024 may well be superseded.

### Salerno city — rank 7, and shaped like Florence

`comune.salerno.it` gives the institutional scaffolding clearly: an **Ufficio Varchi ZTL**
issuing daily permits, an **Ufficio Autorizzazioni** for longer-term ones, where
applications are filed, and a service for checking gate transits and violations.

What it does not give is **the current ordinance or the gate hours**. The only ordinance
located is **n. 291 of 2005**, with Via Portanova under electronic control since December
2007 — historical, not current. Even the permit office's own opening hours are stated
three different ways across three official pages.

So Salerno city is publishable only the way Florence was: explain **why** the hours are
not given, name the office that holds the answer, and refuse to supply numbers. That is a
real page, but it is the weakest of the seven.

**One structural note, not a proposal.** `salerno-airport` is currently parented to
`naples`. If a Salerno city page ever ships, that parenting is worth revisiting — but
reparenting an existing URL needs a written proposal and explicit approval, so it is
flagged here and nothing more.

## 4. Naples ↔ Rome — an owner decision, not a research task

Phase 1 framed this as cannibalisation risk. The codebase says the question is different
and sharper.

**For it:** the homepage already sells this service. It carries a dedicated
**"Intercity transfers"** card — *"City to city in one vehicle: your luggage stays in the
boot, there are no changes along the way"* — and a full band, *"Journeys between cities,
and beyond the border"*. **No intercity route page exists anywhere on the site.** All 18
`/routes/` pages are intra-regional airport-or-port → city hops. So a Naples ↔ Rome page
would not invent a service; it would be the first page to realise one the homepage already
promises.

**Against it, and this is decisive:** that same homepage band ends with

> *"Route availability is confirmed when the journey is quoted, not when it is listed on a
> page. Nothing here states that a given city pair or border crossing is always covered."*

A page at `/routes/naples-to-rome` **lists a city pair on a page**. That is the exact thing
the note was written to avoid. No Rome page mentions Naples, so nothing existing is
cannibalised — the conflict is with our own disclaimer, not with our URLs.

Three outcomes, and only the owner can pick:

1. **Confirm the pair is covered**, and build the page with coverage stated as an owner
   claim through `OwnerNote` — the component Stage 1 added for exactly this kind of fact.
2. **Leave the note as it stands** and build no intercity route pages at all, here or in
   any other region.
3. **Reword the note** so that listing a pair is not a coverage guarantee — a homepage
   content change, which needs its own approval.

Until one of those is chosen, this should not be researched further. The research would be
road distances and durations, and the site publishes neither.

## 5. Still blocked

### Naples Capodichino

**Rechecked today.** `aeroportodinapoli.it` still shows the notice headed *"Naples airport
closed for runway requalification (November 2026)"*, dated **19 Oct**, with a generic "Read
More" and no dedicated closure page. It still publishes **no start date, no end date, no
reopening date, no affected airlines and no alternative airport**.

A page cannot be built on a month. The hub's notice band already carries everything the
operator publishes, with a callout naming what it does not claim, and that remains the
right home for it. Revisit only when the operator publishes dates — and recheck on the day
of writing, not from this audit.

### Amalfi Coast hotel pages

**Defer, unchanged.** Both prerequisites from Bologna remain unmet: **zero Campania hotels
have been researched**, and **no coast destination page exists to parent them**. Bologna
established the cost of a defensible hotel page — the address confirmed on the hotel's own
site, plus a property-specific access fact distinguishing it from its neighbours — and
Bologna also showed what happens without it: two Phase 1 premises were overturned by
verification, and one page had to be corrected for a URL constructed rather than checked.

On the Amalfi Coast the access fact is harder than in Bologna, because for many properties
it is not a ZTL rule but whether a vehicle can physically reach the door at all. That is
not something to infer.

## 6. Recommended sequence

1. **Caserta** — settle the Giannone number, then build. Nothing else is outstanding.
2. **Pompeii** — build with the 2018 parking statement dated in the copy.
3. **Retry ANAS** for the SS163 ordinances. One attempt decides whether the coast block is
   a normal research task or a cluster of declared gaps.
4. **Sorrento**, independent of the SS163 answer — read Ordinanza 230/2023 and the current
   regulation first.
5. **Positano** — read the official portal guide of 20-05-2026. Verify the seat threshold
   or publish nothing about charges.
6. **Amalfi**, then **Ravello** — each after its own ordinance is read.
7. **Salerno city** — only as an explained gap, and only if wanted.
8. **Naples ↔ Rome** — an owner decision first, per §4.
9. **Hotels** — after a coast destination exists and individual properties are verified.

Caserta and Pompeii are also the two that fit the existing IA with no new decisions: both
parent to `naples`, keeping the hub as the regional spine, and both are transfer-endpoint
pages rather than visit guides.

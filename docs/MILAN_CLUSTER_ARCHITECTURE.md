# Private Transfer Across Italy

## Milan & Lombardy Cluster Architecture

**Purpose:** Source of truth for planning and building the
Milan/Lombardy cluster. The Rome cluster is the completed reference for
QA standards, not a template to copy. Build Milan only; do not start
another region until this cluster passes review.

## 1. Strategic objective

Create a useful, accurate, premium bilingual transfer cluster for Milan
and relevant destinations in Lombardy and nearby areas. Connect the
city, its airports, railway stations, hotel areas, business/event
venues, lakes, resorts, and supported intercity/cross-border routes.

SEO, AEO and GEO are quality objectives, not ranking guarantees: -
**SEO:** distinct search intent, useful content, crawlable links,
correct metadata, performance and technical correctness. - **AEO:**
direct answers to real traveler questions. - **GEO:** accurate,
consistent, attributable facts that can be understood by search/AI
systems.

Publish the smallest complete set of genuinely useful pages. Do not
maximize URL count.

## 2. Non-negotiable rules

1.  Milan/Lombardy is the only new cluster in scope until approved.
2.  Preserve the brand **Private Transfer Across Italy**, approved
    fonts, typography scale, colors, global header/footer, route
    registry and project conventions.
3.  Every page needs a distinct, purpose-led composition. Consistent
    brand system; no cloned full-page layouts.
4.  Never create a page solely because a keyword, town, hotel, venue or
    route can become a URL.
5.  Never invent prices, travel times, distances, availability, pickup
    rules, vehicles, licenses, insurance, reviews, affiliations, child
    seats, accessibility, waiting policies or guarantees.
6.  A candidate route is not proof the business can serve it. Verify
    actual provider capability before publishing a service claim.
7.  No fake quote result, booking success, availability checker, price
    calculator, rating or testimonial.
8.  Avoid doorway pages, keyword stuffing, city-name-swapped copy,
    mass-generated near-duplicates and manipulative link blocks.
9.  Use real, relevant, licensed images. Do not present AI images as
    documentary photographs.
10. No public "Image taken from...", "Source: ..." or "Photo credit..."
    captions. Keep provenance in a private asset manifest. If a license
    requires visible attribution, choose a suitable alternative or
    comply with its terms.
11. Preserve English/Italian routing conventions. Italian copy and slugs
    must be natural and consistent with the current route registry.
12. Canonical, reciprocal hreflang, sitemap, breadcrumbs and internal
    links must agree.
13. Do not commit, deploy, change secrets/environment, modify APIs/CRM
    or alter quote behavior without explicit authorization.
14. Audit existing Milan content and URLs before proposing pages. Merge,
    update or defer duplicates.
15. Distinguish general travel information from confirmed service terms.
16. Unique design alone does not guarantee rankings; useful, distinct,
    reliable content is essential.

## 3. Geographic and transport inventory

### Milan's key airports

Research and verify: - Milan Malpensa Airport (MXP) - Milan Linate
Airport (LIN) - Milan Bergamo Airport (BGY), also known as Orio al Serio

Confirm official names, IATA codes, location, operator guidance and
pickup/terminal information from authoritative sources. Clearly
distinguish the airports. Do not imply Bergamo Airport is in central
Milan or that all airports have identical pickup rules.

### Railway stations to assess

-   Milano Centrale
-   Milano Cadorna
-   Milano Porta Garibaldi
-   Milano Rogoredo
-   Other stations only if demand and distinct practical content justify
    them.

Assess whether one useful Milan station-transfers hub is better than
many thin station pages.

### Milan districts and accommodation areas to assess

Examples: Centro Storico/Duomo, Brera, Porta Nuova, Centrale, Navigli,
CityLife, Fiera/Portello, San Siro. These are candidates, not automatic
standalone pages. District pages must contain genuinely useful local
logistics, not merely swapped place names.

### Lombardy and nearby destinations

Assess based on demand and service capability: - Bergamo city - Como and
Lake Como - Bellagio, Varenna, Menaggio, Tremezzina and other specific
lake destinations - Monza - Brescia - Franciacorta - Selected Lake Garda
towns with the relevant lake side clearly specified - Pavia and other
nearby destinations where evidence supports demand.

Lake Como is not one single endpoint. Distinguish towns, hotels/villas,
road access and ferry logistics where relevant. Never imply a road
transfer includes a boat or ferry.

### Cross-border routes

Potential candidates include Lugano and St. Moritz, only if
international service capability is verified. Verify provider capability
and current requirements. Do not invent border, toll, permit or legal
advice.

### Cruise-port relevance

Milan has no seaport. Do not create a "Milan cruise port" page. Consider
routes to real ports such as Genoa, Savona or La Spezia only if user
intent and provider capability are verified. Distinguish the port from
Milan and never invent terminal access rules.

### Business, trade fairs and events

Assess Fiera Milano / Milano Rho Fiera, business districts,
fashion-related events, conferences and other relevant venues. Verify
official venue names, dates and access details. Do not imply official
affiliations. Time-limited event pages need unique information and a
maintenance/retirement plan.

### Weddings and private occasions

Create pages only if a real supported service and unique practical
content exist. Venue pages require verified facts and must not imply a
partnership without evidence.

## 5. Master inventory and standalone-page gate

Every candidate needs: - Stable page ID, English title/URL and Italian
title/URL if approved. - Page type, parent hub, primary intent, audience
and supporting questions. - Associated airports, stations, destinations,
services, venues and routes. - Unique facts/content requirements and
research sources. - Service-capability status, verification source and
date. - Inbound/outbound link targets. - Proposed design composition and
rationale. - Image requirements and asset-manifest ID. -
Canonical/hreflang status, schema if appropriate, priority/status, QA
result.

Statuses: `research`, `proposed`, `approved`, `drafting`, `content-QA`,
`technical-QA`, `ready`, `published`, `needs-update`, `deferred`,
`merged`, `retired`.

Publish a standalone URL only if it has distinct user intent, unique
information, evidence, confirmed or honestly qualified service
capability, a clear linking role, no cannibalization, and a useful next
step. Otherwise merge or defer it.

## 6. Page-type content standards

### Milan destination hub

Explain Milan's transfer context and the three different airports. Help
users choose airport, station, accommodation, business/event and
onward-destination pages. Link to approved airport hubs, station
content, routes and relevant Lombardy destinations. Use Milan-specific
facts, not generic city copy.

### Airport pages

Each page must be specific to MXP, LIN or BGY: - Verify official name,
code, operator, location and current guidance. - Explain relevant
destinations and supported routes. - Provide useful arrival/pickup
planning, without inventing terminal, parking, curbside or waiting
rules. - Explain which information travelers should confirm. - Link to
Milan, towns, routes and guides. - Do not make three copies with airport
names swapped.

### Station page

Assess whether one station hub is enough. Verify relevant
station-specific information. Do not recycle airport content with
"station" substituted.

### Hotel/district pages

Explain real geography and transfer logistics. No page for every hotel
by default. No implied hotel affiliation or pickup permission.

### Business/event pages

Give useful planning advice about schedules, groups, venue access and
return trips where supported. Verify time-sensitive event facts and
provide a review/retirement plan. No implied event organizer
affiliation.

### Wedding/private occasion pages

Provide genuine planning guidance only where the service is supported.
Do not invent fleet, vehicle class, venue partnership or guarantees.

### Day trips

Use accurate itinerary and destination information. Distinguish
suggested itineraries from confirmed inclusions. Do not promise tickets,
guides, boat transfers or waiting time unless verified.

### Route pages

Identify both endpoints, who needs the route, endpoint-specific
logistics, what information is needed for an enquiry, and relevant
constraints. Explain that price/time depend on exact addresses,
date/time, traffic, vehicle and service unless verified data exists.
Link to both endpoints and relevant hubs. Never invent prices, duration,
distance or availability.

### Guides

Each guide answers one distinct practical question and adds value beyond
the commercial page. Avoid multiple articles targeting the same intent.

## 7. Page design differentiation --- critical

Keep the same fonts, typography scale, brand palette, global
header/footer, button behavior, accessibility and responsive principles.
Make each page's composition meaningfully different.

Vary according to purpose: - Hero layout and image placement/crop. -
Section order and information hierarchy. - Editorial vs. functional
modules. - Column count, whitespace and background rhythm. - Route
indexes, airport selectors, checklists, timelines, comparison tables,
destination explorers and FAQs. - Content density and visual
storytelling.

Changing only the title, photo or city name is not enough. Avoid
repeating a generic stack like hero → three cards → icon grid → benefits
→ FAQ → CTA on every page.

Before coding each page: 1. Write a design brief: audience, main
question, key facts, CTA, visual narrative. 2. Propose a unique section
sequence. 3. Compare it with all existing Rome and Milan page layouts.
4. Redesign if it looks like a renamed copy. 5. Reuse low-level
components, not a rigid full-page template. 6. Record the choice and
rationale in `milan-design-differentiation.md`. 7. Compare actual
desktop/mobile renderings with sibling pages.

Possible starting directions (not rigid templates): Milan hub with
airport-choice navigator and destination explorer; Malpensa with arrival
workflow and route matrix; Linate with city/business use cases; Bergamo
with clear geography and onward destination matrix; station hub with
rail-to-road transition; Lake Como with town selector and road/ferry
distinction; event page with schedule-led planning; wedding page with
guest logistics; route page with endpoint comparison; day-trip page with
itinerary timeline.

Consistent design alone does not cause a Google penalty. The real risk
is repetitive, low-value or doorway-like content. Different composition
should improve usability, not create arbitrary inconsistency.

## 8. Image sourcing and licensing

For each page, choose imagery that communicates its actual location or
service context. Verify the exact file, subject, creator, license,
commercial use, modification rights and attribution requirements. Record
these in a private asset manifest.

Rules: - Do not use a Bergamo city photo to imply the airport is
shown. - Do not use a Lake Como photo to imply a specific town/hotel
unless verified. - Avoid competitor-branded vehicles/coaches/logos that
advertise a competitor or imply affiliation. - Never present
AI-generated imagery as a real photograph. - Avoid repeated hero
images. - Optimize images, use responsive sizes, prioritize hero loading
and lazy-load below the fold. - Alt text must describe what is visible,
without keyword stuffing. - Use empty alt for decorative images. - Do
not show public source captions ("Image taken from...", "Source: ...",
"Photo credit..."). If a license requires visible attribution, choose
another asset or comply with the license. - No broken images or
misleading crops.

Manifest fields: `asset_id`, `page_id`, `local_path`, `subject`,
`location_verified`, `source_url`, `creator`, `license_name`,
`license_url`, `commercial_use_allowed`, `modification_allowed`,
`attribution_required`, `attribution_text`, `date_checked`, `alt_text`,
`crop_notes`, `review_status`.

## 9. Research, content, and localization

Use official airport operators/authorities, railway operators,
municipalities, tourism authorities, official venue/event sources, and
confirmed provider records for business capability. Use current
authoritative sources for cross-border/legal/toll claims. Maintain
`milan-research-log.md` with claim, source URL, date checked, pages
using it and caveats.

Content must answer the main question early, use verified facts, explain
uncertainty, and provide practical Milan-specific guidance. Avoid
repetitive marketing filler. Never claim 24/7 service, fixed fares, free
waiting, flight tracking, licenses, insurance, luxury fleet, child
seats, accessible vehicles or guarantees unless verified. Do not
fabricate reviews, credentials, partnerships, customer counts, addresses
or offices.

English and Italian: - Follow existing route conventions. - Italian must
be natural, not word-for-word translation. - Localize titles, metadata,
headings, copy, CTA, forms, FAQs, breadcrumbs, relevant alt text and
schema. - Keep facts consistent. - Reciprocal `hreflang` only for
genuine equivalents; self-canonical on each page. - `x-default` only if
it matches the existing language strategy. - Language switch opens the
counterpart if available; otherwise the correct language hub. - Do not
create low-quality Italian pages just to fill URLs.

## 10. Internal linking

Create `milan-internal-link-map.csv` before release: - Homepage →
destination/service hubs and Milan entry points. - Milan hub → MXP, LIN,
BGY, station hub, approved services, relevant districts and published
routes. - Airport hubs → Milan, relevant towns, routes and guides. -
Station hub → relevant city/district pages and routes. - Lake/town pages
→ relevant airports, Milan and supported routes. - Business/event pages
→ relevant transport hubs and services. - Route pages → both endpoints,
parent hubs and relevant routes. - Guides → service/route pages when
useful. - Every page → accurate breadcrumbs and a useful parent link.

Use natural descriptive anchors. No giant link blocks, draft/dead links
or orphans. Validate all links programmatically.

## 11. SEO, AEO, GEO and technical implementation

For every published page: - Unique title/meta description, one clear H1,
logical headings. - Correct canonical, reciprocal hreflang, sitemap,
robots and status codes. - Accurate Open Graph metadata and crawlable
links. - Accurate breadcrumbs and BreadcrumbList when appropriate. -
Organization/WebSite/WebPage schema only where consistent with the
existing implementation. - LocalBusiness only if real business details
and eligibility justify it; never invent a Milan office. - FAQ schema
only when appropriate and visible answers match. It does not guarantee
rich results. - No fake
ratings/reviews/prices/offers/telephone/address. - Structured data must
match visible content. - Core content should not depend on fragile
client-side rendering. - Give direct answers to real questions; never
promise rankings or AI citations.

## 12. Accessibility and performance

Test at 320, 360, 390, 768, 1024, 1280 and 1680 px. Verify WCAG AA
contrast, keyboard access, focus states, labels, semantic landmarks, tap
targets, responsive image sizes, layout shift, reduced-motion behavior,
mobile-specific composition and CTA clarity.

## 13. Milan delivery phases

**Gate 0 --- Audit:** inspect existing routes/content, homepage, Rome
cluster, shared design system, quote flow, image pipeline, metadata,
sitemap, robots, schema and integrations. Preserve existing behavior;
report conflicts first.

**Gate 1 --- Inventory:** research and score candidates; verify sources,
service capability and image rights. Mark each `publish`, `merge` or
`defer`. Deliver `milan-page-inventory.csv` before bulk coding.

**Gate 2 --- Link plan:** validate URL patterns and bilingual mapping;
deliver `milan-internal-link-map.csv`.

**Gate 3 --- Build core:** Milan hub; MXP; LIN; BGY; station hub if
justified; high-value supported airport routes; selected Lake Como/town
pages; business/event/hotel/wedding/day-trip/cross-border pages only if
distinct and verified; useful guides.

**Gate 4 --- Italian localization:** natural equivalent pages, correct
metadata, canonical, hreflang, breadcrumbs, links and sitemap.

**Gate 5 --- Content/design/image QA:** check claims against research
log, compare page composition, verify asset licensing and alt text.

**Gate 6 --- Technical QA:** build, TypeScript, lint, links, images,
metadata, canonical/hreflang, sitemap, robots, schema, accessibility,
responsive and browser checks. No deployment or commit.

**Gate 7 --- Approval:** report results and wait for user approval
before another cluster.

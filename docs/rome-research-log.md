# Rome cluster — research log

Every factual claim the Rome/Lazio pages make, the source it came from, the
date it was checked, and the pages that depend on it.

**All dates checked: 2026-10-24** unless stated otherwise.

The rule this log exists to enforce: if a claim is not in this table, it does
not go on a page. Where sources disagreed, the disagreement is recorded rather
than resolved by picking the convenient number — and in those cases the pages
either cite the operator's own figure or avoid the figure altogether.

## 2. Rome railway stations — RFI (the infrastructure manager)

Sources: <https://www.rfi.it/en/stations/roma-termini.html>,
<https://www.rfi.it/en/stations/roma-tiburtina.html>

| # | Claim | Used on |
|---|---|---|
| S1 | Roma Termini has 32 tracks serving passenger trains | rome, rome-stations |
| S2 | Roma Tiburtina has 17 tracks serving passenger trains | rome-stations |
| S3 | Both stations' reference Sala Blu (the assistance service for disabled passengers and passengers with reduced mobility) is Roma | rome-stations |
| S4 | Sala Blu assistance between 07:45 and 22:30 can be requested in person or by telephone up to one hour before departure or arrival | rome-stations |
| S5 | Roma Tiburtina's lifts operate 05:00–00:00, and RFI notes that temporary lift outages can affect step-free access | rome-stations |
| S6 | Both stations list bus and metro connections, car rental and car sharing among their integrated mobility services | rome-stations |

**Sources disagree:** Roma Termini's rank among European stations. Wikipedia
calls it Italy's busiest and Europe's fifth-busiest; the consumer association

track count comes from the infrastructure manager and needs no ranking to be
useful.

**Not researched, therefore not published:** Roma Ostiense and Roma San Pietro
beyond their existence as Rome stations. Neither has a standalone page, and
the station page says only what is verified about each.

| # | Claim | Used on |

| P2 | The port lies roughly 70 km north-west of Rome — the port's own site gives 45 miles / 70 km | civitavecchia, rome, route-rome-civitavecchia, route-fco-civitavecchia |
| P3 | Civitavecchia is a separate town from Rome, with its own railway station | civitavecchia, route-rome-civitavecchia |

**Sources disagree:** the number of cruise terminals (four or five, depending
on the page), the distance to Rome (70–80 km across sources), and whether the
Largo della Pace shuttle is free or charged. The pages use the port's own
70 km figure, state the dock numbers rather than a terminal count, and say
nothing about shuttle fares.

**Deliberately not claimed anywhere:** that a private car can reach a specific
berth or gangway. Access inside a working port is controlled, varies by dock
and by ship, and no primary source sets out a rule that applies generally. The
port page says the meeting point must be confirmed, which is the honest answer
and also the useful one.

## 4. Rome's limited-traffic zones (ZTL)

Source: <https://romamobilita.it/muoversi-a-roma/ztl-in-centro/> (Roma Servizi
per la Mobilità, the city's mobility agency)

| # | Claim | Used on |
|---|---|---|
| Z1 | Central Rome has five limited-traffic zones: Centro Storico (day and night), Tridente, Trastevere (day and night), San Lorenzo (night) and Testaccio (night) | rome, rome-stations |
| Z2 | ZTL Centro Storico operates 06:30–18:00 Monday to Friday and 14:00–18:00 on Saturday, public holidays excluded | rome |
| Z3 | The Centro Storico night zone operates 23:00–03:00 on Friday and Saturday and is not active in August | rome |
| Z4 | ZTL Trastevere operates 06:30–10:00 Monday to Saturday, public holidays excluded | rome |
| Z5 | Taxis and private-hire cars are among the categories permitted to enter — the agency's wording is "le categorie di legge, i taxi, le auto a noleggio con conducente (NCC)" | rome |

**Needs re-check — the most volatile facts in this log.** ZTL hours are set by
city ordinance and change, including seasonally: the agency's own page records
a May-to-October extension of the Trastevere night zone, and the city
published a separate summer 2026 timetable for Trastevere and San Lorenzo. The
Rome hub therefore states the hours as *at the time of writing*, dates that
statement, and links to the agency so a reader can check the day's rules.

Z5 describes which **categories of vehicle** are permitted. It is not a claim
about any particular journey, driver or permit, and no page presents it as one.

## 7. What no source could establish

These gaps are the reason several candidate pages are deferred and the reason
every page is framed as an enquiry rather than an offer.

- Which Rome routes, vehicle categories and meeting arrangements the business
  can actually provide. No first-party operational record was available.
- Whether waiting time, flight monitoring, child seats, accessible vehicles or
  named vehicle classes can be offered. Unverified, therefore never mentioned.
- Any price, for any journey, anywhere in the cluster.
- Any journey duration. Several secondary sources offer figures; they
  disagree, they ignore traffic, and the operator publishes none. The pages say
  what the duration depends on instead of inventing one.
- Whether the business holds any affiliation with a venue, hotel, airline,
  cruise line or railway operator. Nothing of the kind is implied anywhere.
